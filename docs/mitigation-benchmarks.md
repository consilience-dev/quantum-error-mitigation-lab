# Benchmarking pretrained regressors against quantum error mitigation methods

The Algiers ML-QEM study establishes a comparison against digital zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) and the paper’s random forest on identical circuit rows. The released hardware results contain four ideal, noisy and ZNE-mitigated observables per circuit. On the authors’ 2,500 test rows, mean circuit L2 was 0.20971 raw, 0.18682 with ZNE, 0.10004 with their forest and 0.05318 with frozen Nori 1.5. Appendix E discusses additional methods and estimates their costs. It supplies no Algiers per-circuit PEC or CDR results. [Verified Algiers results](../reports/RESULTS.md) · [ML-QEM, Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17368v2#A5)

## Method coverage and acquisition requirements

The tutorial names ZNE and quasiprobability methods in sections 2 and 3. At 1:09:03, section 4 lists tensor-network mitigation, postselection, virtual error distillation, learning-based methods including CDR, and matchgates. Different methods need different experimental records for the same ideal quantity. [QIP tutorial](https://qip2026.lu.lv/programme/tutorial/) · [Recording, section 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5aDdF7Y2gww&t=4143s)

| Method | Matched evidence so far | Data needed for the next comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Digital ZNE | Published Algiers hardware and FakeLima simulator estimates scored against pretrained models | Measurements at the published noise scales, extrapolation rule and shots per scale for a cost-controlled replication |
| Quasiprobability (QP) / probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) | Song et al.’s four hardware phases scored against three readout-calibrated frozen regressors. Published hardware PEC scores reproduced in two other studies. | A full-input comparison requires the gate-characterization measurements and target variants on many circuits with varying ideal answers. [Song comparison](../reports/pec_song/README.md) · [Russo results](../reports/pec_russo/README.md) · [Spin-chain results](../reports/pec_spin_chain/README.md) |
| Clifford data regression (CDR) | Qermit supplies target-specific CDR, ZNE and raw estimates on two IBM devices and two simulators. A new depolarizing simulation evaluates frozen regressors and affine CDR on identical per-target inputs. | A matched hardware fit needs the original auxiliary noisy/ideal observations and the query at the CDR shot allocation, or a new hardware acquisition. [Qermit paper](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-07-13-1059/) · [Matched simulator results](../reports/RESULTS.md#qermit-with-20-calibration-runs-per-target) |
| Tensor-network error mitigation (TEM) | Fischer et al. publish raw measurement samples, noise models and kicked-Ising circuit generation. Pretrained comparison pending. | Informationally complete measurement outcomes or a protocol-specific equivalent, characterization of noise, and a documented contraction approximation and classical cost. [TEM method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11740) |
| Symmetry verification / postselection | Coverage pending | A target with a known symmetry sector and joint measurement outcomes or check flags. Track accepted and attempted shots and verify that the observable is compatible with the check. [Symmetry verification](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.98.062339) |
| Virtual error distillation | Coverage pending | Multi-copy measurements or an equivalent randomized-measurement protocol with numerator and normalization data. Count state preparations and measurement configurations. [Virtual distillation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.041036) |
| Matchgate-based approaches | The talk names matchgates and leaves the protocol open | A concrete candidate is training with fermionic linear optics (TFLO) on a Fermi–Hubbard task. Collect known-ideal, noisy fermionic-linear-optics training circuits and pair held-out target observables with a reference. Audit the published combined postselection and TFLO results before attributing a gain to either component. [TFLO method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02120) · [Hardware study and data](https://github.com/PhaseCraft/fhvqe-paper) |

For postselection and virtual distillation, O’Brien et al. provide a hardware study on paired-electron circuits and an archive with raw and processed data. For matchgates, the Fermi–Hubbard archive includes a published TFLO application. Before promising a calibration budget for either, identify the unique target circuits, find paired predictions on the published observable and reproduce the authors’ score. For TEM, Fischer et al. release raw measurements, noise models and circuit-generation code for kicked-Ising experiments reaching 91 qubits. Begin with their analytically checkable dual-unitary correlators, include a parameter-only control and establish how many independent labeled circuits can be held out. [Paired-electron study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-023-02240-y) · [Paired-electron data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7225821) · [Fermi–Hubbard code and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7060071) · [TEM study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-025-03144-9) · [TEM data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29069759)

## Qermit benchmark reproduction

Both hardware devices ran the same 160 target circuit–observable pairs. Include all 100 unmirrored and 60 mirrored targets in their published cells, with five targets per hardware cell. The mirrored ideals equal +1 to numerical precision, so report mirrored and unmirrored results separately as the authors do. The native reference uses matched raw, ZNE and CDR estimates from the deposit. Preserve the authors’ experiment-to-observable mapping, including routed qubit labels for ZNE. [Archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6472281)

For hardware, the notebook allocates 32,000 shots to raw, 32,000 at each of five ZNE scales and 7,619 per CDR circuit (20 auxiliaries and the target). The CDR total is 159,999 after integer rounding, which the paper reports as 160,000. An equivalent pretrained estimator needs the same per-target noisy and ideal auxiliary values and the target measurement from the CDR allocation. The separately archived raw measurement has a different shot budget. The 580 additional unmirrored emulator circuit IDs in the availability audit are unrelated targets and do not supply these auxiliary data. [Archive audit](../reports/qermit_availability.json) · [Authors’ executable protocol](https://zenodo.org/records/6472281/files/example_experiment.ipynb)

We recomputed Figures 7, 8 and 11–14 from the authors’ archived estimates. All 1,536 median and worst-case values agreed with the plotted colors at the published resolution. Across the 20 unmirrored hardware cells per device, CDR lowered the median error relative to raw in 16 cells on Casablanca and 11 on Lagos. ZNE did so in 18 and 6, respectively. The score file retains each cell’s median and worst-case result. Table 4 gives 0.199 for mean raw absolute error on unmirrored random Lagos circuits. The released 50 observations give 0.148771, consistent with the table’s ten width/depth cells. [Source checks](../reports/qermit_reference.json) · [All cell scores](../reports/qermit_reference_cells.csv)

Two replays on the 680 unmirrored depolarizing targets give CDR, Nori 1.5, TabICLv2 and TabPFN v2 the same 20 noisy/ideal auxiliary observations and the same noisy query within each replay. Both follow the notebook’s allocation of 23,809 noisy shots for each auxiliary and query, with a separate 100,000-shot raw reference. Running the acquisition-era Qermit graph recovered CDR improvements over raw in 68 of 68 median-error groups, compared with 67 in the archive. The modern implementation reached 53. The archive lacks the historical auxiliaries and query needed for a matched pretrained hardware fit. ## Hardware PEC and the next comparison

Song et al.’s public figures contain real hardware readout calibration and the original four controlled-phase test means. The three frozen regressors and affine correction use that readout calibration. The paper’s full PEC includes gate-specific characterization. We checked the figure values against the text and a second rendering, and scored every method on all four phases. [Published experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw5686) · [Comparison](../reports/pec_song/README.md)

Strikis et al. measured ten phases on three IBM devices. Their protocol gives both the learned PEC estimator and a regressor the same four Pauli responses. We identified the inputs needed to repeat the comparison, including the 24 calibration measurements and 40 phase/Pauli measurements per device. [Figure 10 data availability and replay protocol](../reports/pec_strikis/README.md) · [PRX Quantum paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.040330)

For a future controlled acquisition, a second, classically tractable family with varying targets, such as small Max-Cut QAOA circuits, would permit independent calibration and test shots. Freeze circuit partitions and run raw, ZNE, target-specific CDR, PEC, affine and the frozen model panel on the same target circuits and observables. For PEC, distinguish a simulator-supplied exact channel from a channel fitted on separate calibration experiments. Charge characterization to the fitted-channel result. The idealized estimator is unbiased when its decomposition correctly represents the implemented operations. Finite samples add variance and miscalibration can introduce bias. [PEC theory and experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-023-02042-2)

Use several total shot budgets and repeat the sampling independently at each. Report bias, variance, mean squared error and a paired accuracy-versus-cost curve. Track calibration and inference shots separately, including noise-scaled circuits, near-Clifford variants and discarded shots. Show amortized cost per target for a stated number of queries, count ideal labels and disclose classical simulation or tensor-contraction costs. For future circuit-specific panels, provide each protocol its required measurements and include an input-matched pretrained arm where feasible.
