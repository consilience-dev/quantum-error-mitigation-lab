# Benchmarking pretrained regressors against quantum error mitigation methods

The Algiers ML-QEM study establishes a comparison against digital zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) and the paper’s random forest on identical circuit rows. The released hardware results contain four ideal, noisy and ZNE-mitigated observables per circuit. On the authors’ 2,500 test rows, mean circuit L2 was 0.20971 raw, 0.18682 with ZNE, 0.10004 with their forest and 0.05318 with frozen Nori 1.5. Appendix E discusses additional methods and estimates their costs. It supplies no Algiers per-circuit PEC or CDR results. [Verified Algiers results](../reports/RESULTS.md) · [ML-QEM, Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17368v2#A5)

## Method coverage and acquisition requirements

The tutorial names ZNE and quasiprobability methods in sections 2 and 3. At 1:09:03, section 4 lists tensor-network mitigation, postselection, virtual error distillation, learning-based methods including CDR, and matchgates. Different methods need different experimental records for the same ideal quantity. [QIP tutorial](https://qip2026.lu.lv/programme/tutorial/) · [Recording, section 4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5aDdF7Y2gww&t=4143s)

| Method | Matched evidence so far | Data needed for the next comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Digital ZNE | Published Algiers hardware and FakeLima simulator estimates scored against pretrained models | Measurements at the published noise scales, extrapolation rule and shots per scale for a cost-controlled replication |
| Quasiprobability (QP) / probabilistic error cancellation (PEC) | A separate hardware archive contains raw, corrected and exact probability vectors for two spin-chain examples | A characterized inverse noise model, sampled circuit outcomes, signs, normalization and shots, or archived PEC predictions linked to many eligible held-out circuits. [PEC implementation](https://mitiq.readthedocs.io/en/stable/guide/pec-3-options.html) |
| Clifford data regression (CDR) | Qermit supplies final target-specific CDR estimates alongside ZNE and raw measurements on two IBM devices | Use the authors’ linked estimates. A fresh CDR fit also needs noisy and exact outputs for near-Clifford variants of **each target**. Generic affine calibration on unrelated labeled circuits answers a different question. [Qermit paper](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-07-13-1059/) |
| Tensor-network error mitigation (TEM) | Fischer et al. publish raw measurement samples, noise models and kicked-Ising circuit generation. Pretrained comparison pending. | Informationally complete measurement outcomes or a protocol-specific equivalent, characterization of noise, and a documented contraction approximation and classical cost. [TEM method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11740) |
| Symmetry verification / postselection | Coverage pending | A target with a known symmetry sector and joint measurement outcomes or check flags. Track accepted and attempted shots and verify that the observable is compatible with the check. [Symmetry verification](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.98.062339) |
| Virtual error distillation | Coverage pending | Multi-copy measurements or an equivalent randomized-measurement protocol with numerator and normalization data. Count state preparations and measurement configurations. [Virtual distillation](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.041036) |
| Matchgate-based approaches | The talk names matchgates and leaves the protocol open | A concrete candidate is training with fermionic linear optics (TFLO) on a Fermi–Hubbard task. Collect known-ideal, noisy fermionic-linear-optics training circuits and pair held-out target observables with a reference. Audit the published combined postselection and TFLO results before attributing a gain to either component. [TFLO method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02120) · [Hardware study and data](https://github.com/PhaseCraft/fhvqe-paper) |

For postselection and virtual distillation, O’Brien et al. provide a hardware study on paired-electron circuits and an archive with raw and processed data. For matchgates, the Fermi–Hubbard archive includes a published TFLO application. Before promising a calibration budget for either, identify the unique target circuits, find paired predictions on the published observable and reproduce the authors’ score. For TEM, Fischer et al. release raw measurements, noise models and circuit-generation code for kicked-Ising experiments reaching 91 qubits. Begin with their analytically checkable dual-unitary correlators, include a parameter-only control and establish how many independent labeled circuits can be held out. [Paired-electron study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-023-02240-y) · [Paired-electron data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7225821) · [Fermi–Hubbard code and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7060071) · [TEM study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-025-03144-9) · [TEM data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29069759)

## Next hardware comparison with Qermit

The release contains the same 100 non-mirrored circuit–observable IDs on both Casablanca and Lagos, with raw, ZNE, CDR and ideal values. These include 50 random circuits and 50 Pauli-gadget circuits. All 60 additional mirrored hardware targets equal +1 to numerical precision. An always-+1 predictor reaches numerical precision on those cases, so score them separately as a diagnostic. The authors report 32,000 shots per raw hardware estimate and 160,000 per ZNE or CDR estimate. It reports median and worst relative error within circuit-family, width and depth cells. [Qermit data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6472281) · [Paper, section 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.09725)

The audit matched 10,560 archived outputs across the two hardware and two simulated backends. It found 580 non-mirrored emulator circuits with distinct serialized circuit structures outside the 100 target IDs. The deposited data support an accuracy comparison at the authors’ shot budgets. The scalar outputs alone cannot reconstruct a shot-matched experiment. [Reproducible audit](../reports/qermit_availability.json)

Before fitting a pretrained model on this release, we propose the following protocol.

1. Reserve the same 100 non-mirrored circuit IDs on **both devices**. Reproduce the authors’ relative-error summaries on their original cells. Preserve their experiment-to-observable mapping. The archive links ZNE’s routed Pauli strings by experiment ID. The audit also checks their Pauli factors. Independently verifying every logical-to-physical qubit permutation would require per-run routing maps. Report rounding or discrepancies before interpreting model deltas.
2. Use 500 of the 580 remaining non-mirrored emulator circuit IDs as a simulator-to-hardware calibration set, with nested 18 and 90 label draws. Use the Lagos emulator as the primary calibration source and report the depolarizing simulator separately as a sensitivity check. Freeze the emulator, sampling seeds, family allocation, input features and preprocessing. Supply hardware raw measurements only at inference. This estimates transfer from simulator calibration. A hardware-calibrated comparison needs a separate grouped split and a smaller label budget.
3. Prespecify frozen Nori 1.5, TabPFN v2 and TabICLv2 as the primary model panel. Score all three against the same raw, ZNE, CDR, affine, forest and constant-prediction controls. Include measurement-only, descriptor-only and combined inputs. Log other checkpoints as exploratory and show their coverage and failures. Reserve tuning decisions for training data.
4. Report paired absolute error and differences on each device and circuit family. Include the paper’s relative metric, with an explicit convention for zero or near-zero raw error. Resample entire circuit IDs, with the two devices kept together for a combined estimate. An archived scalar estimate supports uncertainty across target circuits. Estimating shot variance needs count records or fresh randomized repetitions.

To reproduce the archive audit, run `python3 scripts/audit_qermit.py /tmp/qermit --download --check`. The report includes the published checksums, computed SHA-256 digests and group counts. No pretrained score on Qermit has been computed in this audit.

## A controlled PEC comparison and method-specific extensions

Generate a second, classically tractable family with varying ideal targets, such as small Max-Cut QAOA circuits. Freeze circuit partitions and use independent calibration and test shots. Run raw, ZNE, target-specific CDR, PEC, affine, forest and the frozen model panel on the same target circuits and observables. For PEC, distinguish a simulator-supplied exact channel from a channel fitted on separate calibration experiments. Charge characterization to the fitted-channel result. The idealized estimator is unbiased when its decomposition correctly represents the implemented operations. Finite samples add variance and miscalibration can introduce bias. [PEC theory and experiment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-023-02042-2)

Use several total shot budgets and repeat the sampling independently at each. Report bias, variance, mean squared error and a paired accuracy-versus-cost curve. Track calibration and inference shots separately, including noise-scaled circuits, near-Clifford variants and discarded shots. Show amortized cost per target for a stated number of queries, count ideal labels and disclose classical simulation or tensor-contraction costs. For future circuit-specific panels, provide each protocol its required measurements and include an input-matched pretrained arm where feasible.

One independent public PEC archive contains matched raw, mitigated and exact 16- and 1,024-outcome distributions for four- and ten-qubit states. The release combines PEC with readout mitigation, and the references are exact ground states. These are examples for validating a published calculation. Individual bitstrings from either distribution cannot supply independent calibration and test circuits.
